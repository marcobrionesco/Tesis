\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{otherlanguage}{english}
The Maule Region faces severe water vulnerability affecting agricultural sustainability, particularly sweet cherry orchards (\textit{Prunus avium} L.).
This graduation project develops the engineering design for an experimental wastewater treatment and reuse station using Nature-based Solutions (NbS), located at Frutícola SABA SpA in Linares.
The system is engineered to simultaneously receive and treat three different wastewater sources: domestic wastewater (offices and housing), agrochemical washing effluents, and surface water from the Batuco stream.

The sanitary solution employs a battery of Vertical Subsurface Flow Constructed Wetlands (VF-CW) arranged in an above-ground modular configuration using industrial IBC containers of 1.00~m$^3$~\parencite{dotro2017treatment}.
The filter beds sizing was determined by isolating the organic and ammonia loading parameters through a stoichiometric oxygen balance model~\parencite{platzer1999design}, while the stability of the elevated platform for storage tanks was evaluated by integrating the LRFD method and industrial seismic modeling.

The mass balance determined the necessary active area to meet a conservative organic design loading, guaranteeing a sufficient oxygen transfer rate to oxidize the organic matter from the wastewater flows.
Structurally, regarding the standard seismic demand of the location zone, the steel design preserves global stability, registering flexo-compressive interaction indexes and foundation contact pressures lower than the allowable bearing capacity of the soil.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Constructed wetlands, Nature-based Solutions, Hydraulic design, Structural calculation.
\end{otherlanguage}
